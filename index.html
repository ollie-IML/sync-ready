Sync-ready an artist manager's checklist

Everything from today's session in one place: what sync-ready means, who does what, the tools we looked at, where to check the data, and what to read next.

Four things true for every song if any one is missing, the answer can't be yes, fast

  • ✓Splits are agreed

    Every writer and producer agrees and signs their share in advance. It's clear who owns and collects for the master and for the song. Samples are cleared.

  • ✓Registered, 100%

    Every share is registered with each writer's society, adds up to 100% and matches. ISWC and ISRC are in place before release.

  • ✓Assets are ready

    WAV master, instrumental, stems, clean edit, short cutdowns and lyrics, ready to send in minutes.

  • ✓Yes is fast

    One contact who answers. The artist's approval rules agreed in advance. A sense of the price.

ISRC recording ID · ISWC song ID · IPI writer ID · PRO performing rights organisation, the collecting society (SGAE in Spain, for example)

What supervisors check first the society databases, before anyone calls you

  • Not registeredWho owns it? Too risky to chase.
  • Doesn't add up to 100%An uncleared sample? A missing writer?
  • Shares don't matchA dispute over splits? They move on.

You never hear about the syncs you lose this way. Look your artist's songs up the way a supervisor would.

Who does what in order; the manager doesn't do it all, but owns the outcome

  1. 1 · ContributorsWriters and producers agree the splits in advance.
  2. 2 · ManagerMakes sure everything is registered properly with the relevant parties.
  3. 3 · Sync agentPitches out and brings back the clearance request. Needs authority for every share.
  4. 4 · LabelClears the master use and collects the master's share of the fee.
  5. 5 · PublisherClears the song and collects the publishing share of the fee.

Self-released and self-published? The artist may hold several of these roles. That can be an advantage: one-stop.

Three habits the same gaps that lose a sync also lose royalties

  • From the first sessionSplits agreed in writing: every writer and producer's name, share, society, IPI and publisher.
  • Before releaseRegister, then release. Song registered, ISRC and ISWC linked, assets delivered with the master.
  • Every quarterAudit the catalogue. Check what's actually registered and paid. Old songs are often the ones with gaps.
